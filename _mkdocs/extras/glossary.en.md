# Forex glossary

> An alphabetical dictionary of all terms used in the project. If you encounter an unfamiliar word in the guide — look it up here.

## A

**Ask** — the price at which the broker sells currency to you (the price YOU buy at). Always higher than Bid.

**Ascending** — rising, upward-moving. "Ascending triangle" = a chart pattern with a rising lower trendline and a flat upper resistance.

**Aussie** — slang for AUD/USD.

## B

**Base currency** — the first currency in a pair. In EUR/USD the base = EUR. The rate shows how many USD one EUR costs.

**Bars** — an alternative price display: a vertical line with two "whiskers" (open on the left, close on the right). The equivalent of candlesticks.

**Break-even** — closing a trade at zero (after commissions). Also: "moving the stop to break-even" = shifting the SL to the entry price after price has moved 1R in your favour.

**Bid** — the price at which the broker buys currency from you (the price YOU sell at). Always lower than Ask.

**Bullish** — rising market / sentiment. A bull thrusts its horns upward.

**Backtest** — testing a strategy on historical data.

## C

**Currency pair** — two currencies quoted relative to each other: EUR/USD, GBP/JPY, etc.

**Volatility** — the amplitude of price swings. High volatility = large moves in a short time.

**Uptrend** — a sequence of higher highs and higher lows.

**Win rate** — the percentage of profitable trades. Good benchmark: ≥ 40% with an R:R of 1:2.

## G

**Gap** — a break in price between the close of one candle and the open of the next. Often occurs at the Monday open.

**Head & Shoulders** — a bearish reversal pattern: left shoulder → head (higher) → right shoulder.

**Histogram** — the bar portion of the MACD indicator: the difference between the MACD line and the signal line.

## D

**Double top** — a reversal pattern: price twice fails to break a resistance level, then reverses downward.

**Double bottom** — the mirror image: a reversal pattern at the bottom.

**Demo account** — an account with virtual money on live market quotes. A mandatory stage of learning.

**Deposit** — the amount of money in your trading account.

**Divergence** — a discrepancy between price and an indicator: price makes a new extreme but the indicator does not. A signal of weakening momentum.

**Doji** — a candle where open ≈ close (small body). Signals indecision.

**Long-term** — a horizon from weeks to years.

**Drawdown** — the decline from a balance peak; the maximum loss from a high point.

## E

**ECN (Electronic Communication Network)** — a type of trading account that routes orders directly to the interbank market. Raw spreads plus commission, no broker mark-up.

**EMA (Exponential Moving Average)** — a moving average that gives more weight to recent candles than older ones.

## J

**Trading Journal** — an essential document where you record every trade: entry, exit, reasoning, result, and mistakes.

## Z

**Close** — the last price of a candle / closing a position.

**Order** — an instruction to the broker to open or close a position.

## I

**Impulse** — a strong, directional price move.

**Indicator** — a mathematical function of price that helps with decision-making (RSI, MACD, MA, Bollinger Bands).

**Execution** — the process of placing your order in the market. Can be Market (at market price) or Instant (at a specified price).

## K

**Cable** — slang for GBP/USD.

**Kiwi** — slang for NZD/USD.

**Pin Bar** — a candle with a long wick and a small body — a reversal signal.

**Quote** — the current price of a pair: Bid / Ask.

**Leverage** — the ratio of position size to your own capital. 1:100 = with $100 you control $10,000.

**Cross (Cross pair)** — a pair without USD: EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY, etc.

## L

**Neckline** — the support line in a Head & Shoulders pattern.

**Long** — buying. A bet on the price rising.

**Lockup** — the margin frozen against an open position.

**Lot** — the unit of volume. 1 standard lot = 100,000 units of the base currency.

**Liquidity** — how easily an asset can be bought or sold without significantly moving the price.

## M

**MACD** — a trend and momentum indicator: the difference between a fast and a slow EMA, plus a signal line.

**Market Maker** — a market participant that continuously quotes both Bid and Ask prices. Usually banks.

**Margin** — the amount of money frozen as collateral for an open position.

**Margin Call** — a broker warning: "your free margin is running out — forced closure is approaching."

**Majors** — the most liquid pairs involving USD: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, etc.

**Bearish** — falling market. A bear swipes its paw downward.

**Micro-lot** — 0.01 of a standard lot = 1,000 units of the base currency. The minimum for beginners.

**Mini-lot** — 0.1 of a standard lot = 10,000 units.

**Hammer** — a bullish reversal pattern: small body at the top, long lower wick.

## N

**NFP (Non-Farm Payrolls)** — US employment data excluding the agricultural sector. Released on the first Friday of each month. The most important news release of the month.

**Nonfarm** — the informal name for NFP.

## O

**OHLC** — Open, High, Low, Close — the four prices that make up a candle.

**Pullback / Retracement** — a temporary move against the primary trend.

**Order** — an instruction to the broker: open, close, or modify a position.

**Open** — the first price of a candle / opening a position.

**Overnight** — carrying a position past the daily market close. Accompanied by a swap charge.

## P

**Shooting Star** — a bearish reversal pattern: small body at the bottom, long upper wick.

**Pattern** — a recognisable shape in price movement that has statistical significance.

**Overbought** — RSI > 70. Price has risen too fast; a pullback is possible.

**Oversold** — RSI < 30. The mirror situation.

**Pip** — the standard unit of price change. Usually the 4th decimal place.

**Engulfing** — a candlestick pattern where the body of the second candle completely covers the body of the first.

**Support** — a price level from which price has historically bounced upward.

**Profit Factor** — total profits divided by total losses. Good benchmark: ≥ 1.5.

**Breakout** — a price move through a resistance or support level.

**Drawdown** — the decline in balance from a peak. See also Drawdown in D.

**Profit** — gain; the positive result of a trade.

**Pipette (Point)** — 1/10 of a pip (the 5th decimal place).

## R

**Reversal** — a change in trend direction.

**Retest** — price returning to a broken level to confirm it as support or resistance.

**Risk/Reward (R:R)** — the ratio of risk to potential profit in a trade. R:R 1:2 = you aim for $2 of profit for every $1 at risk.

**Risk management** — controlling risk: position sizing, stops, diversification.

**RSI (Relative Strength Index)** — an oscillator on a scale of 0–100. Measures overbought / oversold conditions.

## S

**Candles (Candlesticks)** — a graphical representation of price: body + wicks.

**Swing** — a local extreme (high or low) on a chart.

**Swap** — the fee for holding a position overnight. Can be positive or negative.

**Setup** — a specific market situation that meets all the rules of a strategy.

**Signal** — an entry point generated by a strategy's rules.

**Scalping** — trading on minute-level timeframes, capturing small profits.

**Moving Average (MA)** — a smoothed price over the last N periods. Types: SMA, EMA.

**Blow-up (Drain)** — the complete loss of a trading account.

**Resistance** — a price level from which price has historically reversed downward.

**Spread** — the difference between Ask and Bid. The broker's built-in fee.

**Stop Loss (SL)** — an order that automatically closes a trade at a predefined loss level.

**Stop Out** — the broker's forced closure of positions when the margin level reaches a critical threshold.

**Swing trading** — a trading style holding positions from a few hours to several days.

## T

**Timeframe (TF)** — the scale of each candle: M1, M5, M15, H1, H4, D1, W1, MN.

**Take Profit (TP)** — an order that automatically closes a trade when a target profit level is reached.

**Candle body** — the rectangle between Open and Close.

**Candle wick (Shadow)** — the lines above and below the body extending to the High and Low.

**Tilt** — a psychological state after a loss: anger, the urge to "get it back," impulsive trades.

**Trend** — a sustained direction in price: up, down, or sideways (range).

**Trend following** — a trading style that trades in the direction of the prevailing trend.

**Pip** — see Pip above.

## U

**Level** — a horizontal price at which reversals have historically occurred.

**Averaging down** — adding to a losing position hoping for a reversal. **A dangerous practice — beginners must not do this.**

## F

**Fibonacci** — retracement levels at 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%.

**Flag** — a trend-continuation pattern: a strong impulse move followed by a brief consolidation.

**Flat (Range)** — sideways price movement with no clear trend.

**FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)** — the fear of missing a move. Causes traders to enter without a valid signal.

**Forex (FX)** — the foreign exchange market where currencies are traded.

**Fundamental Analysis (FA)** — analysis of macroeconomic factors: central bank rates, inflation, employment, GDP.

## H

**Hedging** — opening an opposing position to protect against risk.

**Harami** — a candlestick pattern: a small candle contained within the body of a larger one.

**Hedge fund** — an institutional investor using complex strategies.

## C (continued)

**Target** — the take-profit level.

**Entry price** — the price at which a position is opened.

**Exit price** — the price at which a position is closed.

## Ch

**Checklist** — a list of mandatory conditions to verify before opening a trade.

## Sh

**Short** — selling. A bet on the price falling.

**Noise** — random price fluctuations that carry no information.

## E (continued)

**Expiry** — for options and futures: the date on which the contract expires.

**Exposure** — the total volume of open positions.

**ECN account** — see ECN.

**Equity** — the current account balance plus the floating profit or loss of open trades.

**Equity curve** — a chart of how equity changes over time.

**Expectancy** — the expected result per trade: (Win rate × Avg Win) − (Loss rate × Avg Loss).
