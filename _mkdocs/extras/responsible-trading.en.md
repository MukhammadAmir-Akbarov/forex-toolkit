# Responsible Trading: When Trading Becomes Gambling

> ⚠️ Educational material, not medical or financial advice.
> If you recognise yourself here — talk to someone you trust or a professional.

!!! danger "Honest truth: trading can be addictive"
    For a beginner, trading often works like a **casino**, not a profession.
    The same mechanisms — excitement, the hope of "getting even," adrenaline — as in slot
    machines. This is not a character weakness: it is how the brain works. Knowing this is
    the first step towards protecting yourself.

This section is not about strategies. It is about making sure the market does not destroy
your life, finances, and relationships. Read this **before** you open a live account.

## Why the Brain Reacts Like a Slot Machine

The market delivers **variable reinforcement**: a win arrives unpredictably. This exact
reward pattern creates the strongest craving — it is also the foundation of casinos and
lotteries. Every profitable trade releases a dopamine spike, and the brain starts chasing
the next one, **ignoring losses**. That is how overtrading and revenge trading begin.

## 🔍 8 Signs That Trading Has Become a Problem

Be honest. **Three or more "yes" answers — it is time to stop.**

- [ ] I trade **more than I planned** and cannot close the terminal.
- [ ] After a loss I immediately open a new trade to **get even**.
- [ ] I increase my lot/risk to recover losses faster.
- [ ] I trade with money I **cannot afford to lose** (rent, loans, borrowed funds).
- [ ] I **hide** the size of my losses from family or downplay them.
- [ ] Trading is affecting my sleep, job, studies, or relationships.
- [ ] I feel anxious or irritable when I am **not** trading.
- [ ] I have already promised myself "one last time" — and broke that promise.

!!! warning "Revenge trading is the most dangerous trap"
    After a loss the urge to recover money immediately is overwhelming — and the trader
    takes double the risk "on emotion." This almost always leads to **even bigger losses**
    and a downward spiral. Rule: after a stop-loss — **close the terminal until tomorrow**.

## 🛡️ Self-Protection Rules

- **Only money you can afford to lose.** Never trade with money earmarked for food, loans,
  or borrowed from others. A loss must not change your life.
- **Daily loss limit.** Lost X% in a day → stop until tomorrow, no exceptions.
- **Trade count limit.** For example, no more than 3 trades per day — a brake against impulse.
- **Time-out after a losing streak.** 3 losses in a row → pause for 24 hours.
- **Demo for at least 3 months.** Build skill first, then put in money.
- **Trade and emotion journal.** Record not just the numbers but also your state —
  tilt shows up on paper. See the [Anti-Tilt Protocol](anti-tilt-protocol.md).
- **Do not trade when tired, angry, in debt, or "to earn money urgently."**

## 🆘 If You Recognise Yourself

1. **Stop depositing.** The single most important action — stop putting money in.
   Withdraw what remains, remove your card from the platform.
2. **Take a break.** Not "I'll quit tomorrow" — a full stop **right now**, for at least
   several weeks. Delete the trading app from your phone.
3. **Tell someone close.** Addiction thrives in secrecy. One honest conversation with
   family or a friend relieves half the pressure.
4. **See a professional.** A psychologist or therapist works with addictive behaviour —
   there is nothing shameful about it; it is effective.
5. **Replace the ritual.** Cravings are often tied to boredom or stress. Sport, walks,
   hobbies fill the same niche without financial risk.

!!! info "Where to find help"
    Specialised help specifically for "trading addiction" is limited, but general
    resources work just as well:

    - **Psychologist / therapist** — approach it as any behavioural addiction
      (the mechanism is the same as problem gambling).
    - **Helplines and crisis lines** — check current contacts for your city or local clinic.
    - **People close to you** — family support statistically increases the chance of recovery.

    Addiction is treatable. The sooner you acknowledge the problem, the easier it is.
