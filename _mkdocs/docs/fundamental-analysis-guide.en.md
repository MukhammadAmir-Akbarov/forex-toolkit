# 🌍 Fundamental Analysis — Reading the Market Through Economics

!!! abstract "What is fundamental analysis"
    Technical analysis answers "**where**" — which level to enter. Fundamental analysis answers
    "**why**" — what drives price in the first place. You don't need to be an economist:
    understanding 5–6 key concepts explains 80% of market moves.

> **Educational material.** Data and examples are historical, for illustrative purposes.

## 1. What Drives a Currency — Three Sources

### Source 1: Interest rates and rate expectations

The most important factor. Money flows to where the yield is higher.

```
Fed Funds Rate = 5.5%
ECB Rate = 4.0%
→ Dollar is more profitable to hold → USD stronger than EUR
→ EUR/USD under pressure
```

### Source 2: Economic growth

Strong economy → companies invest → local currency needed → exchange rate rises.

### Source 3: Risk appetite (Risk On / Risk Off)

When investors are confident → they buy "risky" assets (high-yield currencies, stocks).
When fearful → they flee to "safe" assets.

| Scenario | Risk ON | Risk OFF |
|---|---|---|
| Strengthens | AUD, NZD, CAD, EUR | USD, JPY, CHF |
| Weakens | USD, JPY, CHF | AUD, NZD, high-yield |
| Trigger | Positive data, rising stocks | Recession, crisis, geopolitics |

## 2. Interest Rates — The Main Driver

### Carry Trade — the core idea

```
Borrow JPY at 0.1%
Buy AUD-denominated assets yielding 4.5%
Return = 4.4% annually (without exchange rate change)
```

When millions of traders do this — AUD rises against JPY.
When fear (crisis) → everyone "closes carry" → AUD crashes, JPY surges.

### Interest rate differential and exchange rate

```
EUR/USD and rates (simplified):
2022–2023: Fed raised rates faster than ECB
→ differential: 5.5% (USD) vs 4.0% (EUR) = +1.5% in USD favour
→ EUR/USD fell from 1.11 to 0.96 in 2022
2024–2025: Fed started cutting faster than ECB
→ differential narrows → EUR/USD recovered
```

### Real vs Nominal rates

More important than the nominal rate is the **real rate** (= nominal rate − inflation):

```
US rate = 5.5%, inflation = 3.5% → real rate = +2.0% (attractive)
Japan rate = 0.1%, inflation = 2.5% → real rate = −2.4% (unattractive)
→ USD stronger than JPY on real yield basis
```

## 3. The Central Bank Cycle

Central banks move in cycles — and this is **predictable**.

```
Inflation rises → CB RAISES RATES → currency strengthens
Rates weigh on economy → PAUSE / WAIT → consolidation
Growth slows, unemployment rises → CB CUTS RATES → currency weakens
Stimulus helps → INFLATION RISES → back to beginning
```

### How the CB signals in advance

CBs **almost never** take decisions suddenly (except in crises).
They prepare the market through **forward guidance** — hints in speeches and statements.

Key words in CB rhetoric:

| Hawkish | Dovish |
|---|---|
| "concerned about inflation" | "monitoring economic weakness" |
| "further hikes may be needed" | "ready to cut if needed" |
| "data-dependent" (no cut promise) | "accommodative policy" |
| "well above target" | "inflation coming down" |

!!! tip "Read not just decisions, but minutes too"
    **FOMC Minutes** are released 3 weeks after the decision. They contain discussion details,
    member votes, and forecast nuances. Often move the market more than the rate decision itself.

## 4. How to Read the Economic Calendar

### Event entry structure

```
Friday, June 7, 13:30 UTC
🔴 USD  Non-Farm Employment Change
         Previous: 175K
         Forecast:  185K
         Actual:    272K  ← appears at release
```

### Event analysis algorithm:

1. **Which country?** → Directly affects its currency.
2. **What indicator?** → Labour? Inflation? Growth?
3. **Trend of the indicator** — improving or deteriorating over the past 3–4 months?
4. **Forecast vs actual** — the surprise matters more than the absolute value.
5. **Prior revision** — the previous value is often revised; markets notice this.

## 5. Inflation and Its Effect on Forex

### Key inflation indicators

| Indicator | Country | What it measures | Importance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CPI** (Consumer Price Index) | US, EU, UK | Consumer basket prices | 🔴🔴🔴 |
| **Core CPI** | US | CPI ex food and energy | 🔴🔴🔴 |
| **PCE** (Personal Consumption Expenditures) | US | Fed's favourite indicator | 🔴🔴 |
| **PPI** (Producer Price Index) | US | Wholesale prices (leading → CPI) | 🟠🟠 |

### How inflation affects exchange rates

```
Inflation rises (above 2% target) → CB likely to raise rate → currency strengthens
Rate hike → currency more attractive → rate rises

Inflation falls (below 2%) → CB considers cutting → currency weakens
Rate cut → currency less attractive → rate falls
```

!!! warning "Paradox: high inflation = currency strengthens short-term"
    Short-term: high inflation = CB will hike = currency rises.
    Long-term: high inflation = purchasing power erosion = currency weakens.
    **Forex trades on expectations** — what matters is WHERE inflation is heading,
    not where it is now.

## 6. Labour Market — NFP and Unemployment

### Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP)

Released **first Friday of each month, 13:30 UTC**.
Shows **how many jobs were created** in the month (excluding agriculture).

```
> 200K new jobs → strong labour market → Fed less incentive to cut → USD rises
< 100K → weak market → Fed may cut → USD weakens
```

### Average Hourly Earnings

Often more important than NFP! Wages → consumer spending → inflation.
Wage growth above 4% year-over-year = inflationary pressure = Fed not in a hurry to cut.

## 7. GDP — Reflection of Economic Health

**Gross Domestic Product** — total value of everything produced in a country.

### Released three times for one quarter:

```
GDP Advanced Estimate  → first, preliminary (most important)
GDP Second Estimate    → one month later, with revisions
GDP Final Estimate     → two months later, rarely moves markets
```

### Interpretation

```
GDP > 3% year-over-year → strong growth → attracts investors → currency strengthens
GDP 1–2% → moderate growth → neutral
GDP < 0% for two consecutive quarters → technical recession → currency weakens
```

## 8. What Drives Which Currency: Quick Reference

| Currency | Main drivers |
|---|---|
| **USD** | Fed rate, NFP, CPI, GDP, geopolitics (safe haven) |
| **EUR** | ECB rate, Eurozone CPI, Germany PMI (largest economy) |
| **GBP** | BoE rate, Brexit aftermath, UK CPI and retail sales |
| **JPY** | BoJ rate (traditionally 0%), risk appetite (safe haven), interventions |
| **CHF** | Risk appetite (safe haven), SNB rate, geopolitics |
| **AUD** | RBA rate, commodity prices (iron ore, coal), China economy |
| **NZD** | RBNZ rate, dairy prices, risk appetite |
| **CAD** | Oil (WTI), BoC rate, US–Canada trade |

### Commodity Currencies (AUD, NZD, CAD)

These currencies **correlate with commodity markets**:

```
AUD/USD and iron ore price: correlation ~0.7
CAD (USD/CAD inverted) and WTI crude: correlation ~0.75
```

If oil rises → CAD usually strengthens → USD/CAD falls.

## 9. Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)

PPP states that in the long run **exchange rates should equalise prices of identical goods**
across countries.

### Big Mac Index (The Economist)

Humorous but functional:
```
Big Mac in USA: $5.69
Big Mac in UK: £4.29 (~$5.41)
Conclusion: GBP slightly undervalued relative to PPP
```

!!! warning "PPP is a long-term tool"
    PPP explains moves over 3–10 years but is **useless for trading** weeks.
    A currency can be "overvalued" by PPP for years while rates support capital inflows.

## 10. Combining Fundamentals with Technicals

The best setup — when **both analyses agree**:

```
Fundamentals say: USD should rise (Fed hawkish, CPI high)
Technicals say: EUR/USD at key resistance, RSI overbought
→ Look for SELL EUR/USD from resistance level
→ Fundamentals provide "tailwind", technicals provide entry point
```

### Time horizon for fundamentals

| Horizon | Tool |
|---|---|
| Long-term (months) | Rates, GDP, CB cycle |
| Medium-term (weeks) | CPI, NFP, PMI |
| Short-term (days/hours) | CB speeches, individual data releases |
| Ultra-short (minutes) | Technical analysis |

!!! tip "'Don't fight the wind' rule"
    If fundamentals say "USD rising this quarter", **don't look for long-term EUR/USD longs**.
    Short-term corrections are tradeable, but the main trend is against you.
