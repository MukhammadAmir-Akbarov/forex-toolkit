    Trading Psychology — The Most Important Skill¶

    Mark Douglas quote: "90% of trading results come from psychology, 10% from strategy."

    You'll master technical analysis in a month. Psychology takes years. This document is about that.

    1. Why psychology matters more than technique¶

    Imagine: you have a profitable strategy with a 50% win rate and R:R 1:2. Over 100 trades, that's +50R of profit (50 wins × 2R − 50 losses × 1R = +50R). Risking 1% of your account, that's +50% per series.

    But this only works if you don't break your rules.

    What ruins the result: - Closed a winning trade early out of fear = got +0.5R instead of +2R - Moved your stop further "to give it a chance" = got −3R instead of −1R - Entered without a signal out of FOMO = added a random loss

    One emotional month destroys six months of disciplined work. It's math.

    The key idea¶

    A profitable trader ≠ someone who's always right. A profitable trader = someone who consistently follows the rules, even when they don't feel like it.

    2. Six cognitive biases of a trader¶

    Bias What it is How it shows up
    Confirmation bias You look for evidence that supports your idea Trade is losing → you search for reasons "it'll still turn around"
    Loss aversion The pain of a loss is stronger than the joy of an equal gain You close +1R early, but hold −3R for ages
    Recency bias Recent experience dominates your perception After 3 winning trades you think "I'm a master" and raise risk
    Sunk cost fallacy You can't let go of what you've already spent "I already lost $50, I'll add more to win it back"
    Overconfidence You overestimate your own abilities "I know where the market is going" instead of "I work with probabilities"
    Hindsight bias After the fact, everything seems obvious "I should have entered at the bottom, it was clearly there!"

    These biases are hardwired into our brains by evolution. No one is entirely free of them. The task is to recognize them in the moment and not act on them.

    3. Tilt — the main account killer¶

    Tilt is the state after a loss when: - You're boiling inside, you want to "get even" - You consciously break your rules - You open trades of a larger size - You enter without a signal - You move stops further out

    The physiology of tilt¶

    After a loss, the body releases cortisol (the stress hormone). Cortisol: - Narrows your field of vision - Amplifies the "fight or flight" response - Suppresses rational thinking

    That's why you can't just "pull yourself together." It's brain chemistry.

    Signs that tilt is coming¶

    • ⚠ Your heart is pounding after a loss
    • ⚠ You want to open the next trade immediately
    • ⚠ You ignore your checklist
    • ⚠ You raise your position size "to win it back faster"
    • ⚠ You drop down to lower timeframes

    How to get out of tilt¶

    The best way is not to get in. Rule: 3 losses in a row → stop for the day. No exceptions.

    If you're already in tilt: 1. Close the terminal immediately 2. Go for a walk for 30–60 minutes 3. No charts, no Telegram 4. Write in your journal: "I was in tilt after X" 5. Don't trade until tomorrow

    An account in tilt loses money 10 times faster than it earns it. This is the most expensive emotion.

    4. FOMO — fear of missing the move¶

    FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) is the fear of missing a move.

    What it looks like¶

    • You see EUR/USD spike up without you
    • "Everyone is making money and I'm on the sidelines!"
    • You enter at the end of the move without a signal
    • The next candle reverses — loss

    Why it's deadly¶

    When you enter on FOMO, the move has already exhausted itself. Everyone who wanted to buy has bought. What comes next is a pullback. You're entering at the worst possible price.

    The cure¶

    There will always be enough trades.

    The market runs 5 days a week, 24 hours a day. Thousands of setups a year. Missing one move means nothing.

    Rule: if you didn't flag the signal BEFORE the move started — it's not your trade, no matter what happens.

    5. Greed — closing in profit too early¶

    Paradox: greed more often shows up as exiting too early, not as "trying to grab more."

    The scenario¶

    The trade hits +1R. You think: - "What if it reverses? Better close, $5 is $5" - "I waited a long time for profit, finally I'll take it"

    You close. The price moves another +1R to your TP. Instead of +2R you got +1R. You made −50% of the planned profit.

    Why it's greed¶

    Paradox: you greedily grab the small profit now because you're afraid it'll be taken away. Greed for immediate reward destroys long-term results.

    The cure¶

    TP is set before the trade. You can't move it in profit. You can't close it manually.

    If 5 trades in a row reverse right before TP — review your TP in the strategy, not on the fly.

    6. Fear — hesitation and random stops¶

    How it shows up¶

    • You see a perfect setup but don't click "buy"
    • When you do click — you close immediately at −5 pips
    • You place your stop too tight "so it doesn't hurt"
    • Or too wide "to give the trade a chance"

    The root of fear¶

    The pain of a loss is 2–2.5 times stronger than the joy of an equal profit (loss aversion, Kahneman's research).

    Subconsciously you don't want to feel the loss, so you: - Don't open the trade so you don't risk at all - Close immediately to lock in a minimal loss - Place a wide stop "so it doesn't get hit" (an illusion)

    The cure¶

    1. Reduce your position size to where the loss feels comfortable
    2. Treat every loss as "the cost of a ticket to the game"
    3. Re-read the statistics: at R:R 1:2 and 40% win rate, you're profitable
    4. Before the trade, say out loud: "I'm ready to lose $X, this is part of the process"

    7. Confirmation — looking for reasons not to close¶

    You're in a trade. The trade is going against you. Stop is close.

    What the typical trader does: opens Twitter / YouTube / TradingView ideas and looks for analysis that confirms his position.

    "Look, this expert says EUR is going up. So I'm right. I'll move the stop."

    Why this is a disaster¶

    1. You've already made the decision — you're not looking for truth, you're looking for justification
    2. For every opinion there's an opposite one — you'll pick the one you need
    3. You moved the stop once — you'll move it a second time when it stops working again

    The cure¶

    A stop is a contract with yourself, signed before the trade. Breaking the contract = disaster. Don't break it. Ever.

    If you want to change your mind in the moment — step away from the terminal for 10 minutes. If after that you still want to change your mind — close the trade manually right now (don't move the stop). And log it in your journal as a "rule violation."

    8. Illusion of control¶

    It seems like you control the market if: - You stare at the chart for a long time - You open lots of tabs with analysis - You know all the indicators

    In reality you only control: - Your decision whether to enter or not - Your position size - Your stop-loss and take-profit levels - The decision to close manually (if there's no stop yet)

    What you do NOT control¶

    • Where the price will go
    • What % of your trades will be profitable
    • When stops will be triggered
    • The emotions of other participants

    Accepting uncertainty¶

    A professional trader thinks not "where will the market go," but "if the market goes here — what will I do, if it goes there — what will I do."

    This is probabilistic thinking.

    9. The concept of probabilistic thinking¶

    Every trade is essentially a coin flip with 50/50 odds (roughly). But at R:R 1:2, it's a coin where heads pays $2 and tails takes $1.

    100 flips of such a coin: - Expected profit: +$50 - But you might get 60 heads in a row → +$120 - Or you might get 10 tails in a row at the start → −$10 for the series

    You don't know where in the distribution you are.

    Principles of probabilistic thinking¶

    1. Every trade is equally important — and equally unimportant. Don't celebrate a profit, don't get crushed by a loss. It's one data point in a large distribution.
    2. A good trade is one taken by the rules, regardless of outcome. A bad one is a rule violation, even if it accidentally turns a profit.
    3. Think in series of 30–50 trades, not individual trades.
    4. Don't personalize the market. The market didn't "take your money." The market just moved. It's statistics.

    10. How to train discipline¶

    Discipline is a skill that you train like a muscle.

    Level 1: External structure¶

    • ✅ Print out the checklist (extras/checklist-printable.md) and hang it nearby
    • ✅ Before every trade, physically tick the boxes
    • ✅ A trade with empty boxes doesn't get opened
    • ✅ After the trade, fill in the journal immediately

    Level 2: Limits that protect you from yourself¶

    • ✅ Your broker lets you set a daily loss limit. Set it to 3% of your account. The broker will automatically close everything when it's hit.
    • ✅ Set a maximum number of trades per day: 3–5
    • ✅ Use a separate account for trading, don't mix it with personal money

    Level 3: Habits¶

    • ✅ The same trading hours every day (e.g., 14:00–16:00)
    • ✅ The same workspace (used only for trading)
    • ✅ No distracting windows during the session (Telegram, YouTube)

    Level 4: Rituals¶

    • ✅ Before the session: 5 minutes to review charts, news, checklist
    • ✅ After the session: 5 minutes to write in the journal
    • ✅ Before bed: review the day's trades, notes on emotions

    11. Mental Game — a trader's daily routine¶

    Sleep¶

    • Minimum 7 hours. Lack of sleep undermines discipline more than any market factor.

    Nutrition¶

    • Don't trade hungry or after a heavy meal
    • Minimal coffee while in a trade (increases anxiety)

    Exercise¶

    • 30 minutes of activity a day. Cardio lowers cortisol.

    Alcohol¶

    • Never while trading. After a bad session — even less so.

    Rest¶

    • 1–2 days a week without charts. No exceptions. Your brain needs to switch off.

    Social media¶

    • Delete Telegram channels with "signals"
    • Delete Twitter accounts of emotional analysts
    • Subscribe to 2–3 calm sources (Forex Factory news, educational resources)

    12. When NOT to trade¶

    States of mind¶

    • 😡 Angry, irritated
    • 😰 Anxious, nervous
    • 😴 Sleepy, tired
    • 🎉 Euphoric after a big win
    • 💔 Life stress (breakup, job loss)

    External conditions¶

    • 📅 The day right after a significant profit or loss
    • 📅 Friday evening (low liquidity, gap risk)
    • 📅 Holidays
    • 📅 Before / during / after major news (NFP, FOMC)

    Technical conditions¶

    • 📊 Market in a strong range (for trend strategies)
    • 📊 Abnormally high spread (often during the Asian session and before close)
    • 📊 Unstable internet connection

    Rule: if you have any doubt — skip it. There will always be enough trades.

    13. Practical exercises¶

    Exercise 1: "Emotion diary"¶

    In your trade journal, add a field "Emotion before the trade" (0–10 calmness scale). After a month, look at it — is there a correlation between low scores and losses?

    Exercise 2: "5-second pause"¶

    Before pressing the "Open trade" button — count to 5 out loud. If any doubt comes up in those 5 seconds, don't open it.

    Exercise 3: "Write the scenario"¶

    Before the trade, write down on paper: - What happens if it hits +TP? - What happens if it hits −SL? - What will I do if the price gets stuck in the middle?

    If you don't have a clear answer to any of these, don't open the trade.

    Exercise 4: "One day — one trade"¶

    In your first month, trade a maximum of 1 trade per day. If it's a loss, that's it, the day is over. This trains patience.

    Exercise 5: "Checklist for 30 days"¶

    For 30 days in a row, physically tick the boxes on the checklist. No boxes — no trade. This builds the habit.

    Exercise 6: "Video recording"¶

    Once a week, record a video review of your trades (just for yourself). Speaking your mistakes out loud is more powerful than writing them down.

    Key ideas in one table¶

    If you want to… Do this
    Not blow up on tilt 3 losses in a row → stop for the day
    Not enter on FOMO Signal flagged BEFORE the move, or no trade
    Not close too early TP is set before the trade and doesn't move
    Not move the stop Stop = a contract with yourself
    Lose calmly Position size = a risk that doesn't hurt
    Act like a professional Think in series, not individual trades
    Not burn out Sleep, exercise, days off are mandatory

    Final quote¶

    "We all want to enter at the perfect spot, exit at the top, and never take a loss. That's impossible. But what is possible is to follow your rules and be profitable over the long run. That is mastery."

    — Mark Douglas, The Disciplined Trader
