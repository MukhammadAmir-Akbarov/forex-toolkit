    Video Script #1: "What Is Forex and Why 80% Lose"¶

    Full on-camera text — ~8 minutes. Can be read directly as a teleprompter.

    🎬 TIMESTAMPS¶

    • 0:00–0:45 — Introduction and goal of the video
    • 0:45–2:30 — What is Forex
    • 2:30–5:00 — Why most people lose money
    • 5:00–7:00 — What a profitable trader does
    • 7:00–8:00 — Conclusion

    🎥 SCRIPT (read with intonation)¶

    [0:00 — START, LOOK INTO THE CAMERA]¶

    Hi. My name is [name]. I've been studying forex trading for [N weeks / months], and today I want to share the most important thing I've understood in that time.

    This video is not a course, not a "secret strategy," and not a call to trade. It's an honest breakdown: what forex really is and why 80 percent of people who come here lose their money.

    If you're only thinking about getting started — watch this video to the end before you open an account. I might just save you a thousand dollars and six months of your life.

    Let's go.

    [0:45 — WHAT IS FOREX]¶

    First, in plain language: what is forex.

    Forex is the currency exchange market. The largest financial market in the world. Every day, seven and a half trillion dollars flows through it.

    For comparison: Apple's entire market capitalisation is around three trillion. And on forex, that kind of money moves between currencies every single day.

    [show an EUR/USD chart on screen]

    Look at this. This is the EUR/USD chart — the most popular pair. When I buy this pair, I'm betting that the euro will rise against the dollar. When I sell — that the dollar will strengthen against the euro.

    You profit on the difference in rates. Bought at 1.0850, price moved up to 1.0900 — sold, made 50 pips. Sounds simple, right? That's exactly why most people come here.

    But who actually moves this market?

    [show a list or just tell it]

    — Central banks — manage currency rates and interest rates — Commercial banks — the main market makers, trading in billions — Hedge funds — algorithms, research, information — Corporations — hedging their currency risks — Retail traders — you and me. That's less than 5 percent of the entire market.

    Think about it: you're entering an arena where 95 percent of the money is moved by professionals with million-dollar budgets for technology and analysis. And from that you hope to earn a profit.

    [2:30 — WHY PEOPLE LOSE]¶

    Now for the uncomfortable part. Why do most people lose?

    I found the statistics. According to the European regulator ESMA, between 74 and 89 percent of retail traders lose money. That means more than 8 out of 10.

    According to NFA in the USA — the average lifespan of a retail forex account is four months. After four months the account hits zero.

    [pause, look directly into the camera]

    And do you know what the most interesting part is? It's not because technical analysis doesn't work. And it's not because brokers are scammers. Most brokers are normal, regulated companies.

    People lose for 5 reasons, and I'll break each one down.

    Reason one: emotions. A person opens a trade — it goes into the red — they start to panic. They move the stop further "to give it a chance." Price drops further. They move it again. In the end, 5 pips of risk turns into 50 pips of loss. One episode like that — and a month of profit is wiped out.

    Reason two: high leverage. Brokers offer leverage up to 1:500. On a $100 deposit you can open a position worth $50,000. Sounds great? It means a move of 0.2% against you — and your deposit is gone. Professionals use leverage of 1:3 or 1:10. Not 500.

    Reason three: no risk management. A person opens a trade for 20 percent of their deposit. Trade goes red — lost 20 percent. Opened a second — another 20. After five losing trades the deposit is wiped out. The correct approach is to risk no more than 1 percent in a single trade.

    Reason four: trading on "signals" from Telegram. Thousands of channels promise "100 percent profit." It doesn't work. If someone really had a system with 100 percent profit — they would not be selling signals for $50 a month. They would trade it themselves and make billions.

    Reason five: wanting quick money. "I'll buy euro, it'll go up by evening, I'll earn enough for a phone." A month later that person's account is zero, and the debt is still there. Forex is not a casino. It's a profession, and it takes years to learn it.

    [5:00 — WHAT A PROFITABLE TRADER DOES]¶

    Okay, so what do the 15 percent who stay in the green after a year actually do?

    They do exactly the opposite of what most people do.

    One. They don't trade every day. They wait for good setups. If there was no signal all week — they don't open a trade "just because they're bored."

    Two. They always set a stop-loss. Not "when I remember," not "when I'm scared." Always. Immediately. At the moment of opening the trade.

    Three. They risk 0.5–1 percent of their deposit in a single trade. Not 5%, not 10%, not 20%. One percent. That means even 10 losses in a row is only 10 percent of the deposit. Recoverable.

    Four. They keep a journal. Of every. Single. Trade. No exceptions. They record: date, pair, direction, reason for entry, result, what emotions were present, what mistakes were made. Once a week they review: what patterns keep repeating?

    Five. They start on demo first. At least 3 months. Not one week, not two. Three months, in profit, following the rules — then a small live deposit.

    [pause]

    Sounds boring, doesn't it? No "secret." No "genius move." That's the answer to why 80 percent lose. Because it's boring. And the "genius move" is just excitement.

    [7:00 — CONCLUSION]¶

    So, here's what I want you to take away from this video.

    Forex is not quick money. Not passive income. Not "quit your job and trade from the beach." It's a profession, and people spend one to three years learning it before reaching a stable profit.

    If you're willing to study for at least a year, keep a journal of every trade, and trade with discipline — you have a chance to be in that 15 percent. If you want to "quickly earn money for a new iPhone" — close this video and don't open an account. You'll save yourself money, nerves, and time.

    And one last thing. Read the books first, then open a demo. Not the other way around. Never trade with money you need to live on. Never take out a loan for trading. Never believe promises of easy money.

    Thanks for watching. Good luck.

    [end, stop recording]

    📝 PRE-RECORDING CHECKLIST¶

    ☐ Read the script out loud 2 times
☐ TradingView open with EUR/USD on a second screen
☐ Wrote down the ESMA statistic (74–89%) on paper
☐ Timed a draft recording — should be ~8 minutes
☐ Quiet room
☐ Glass of water nearby
☐ Phone/camera battery > 50%

    🎤 DELIVERY TIPS¶

    • Don't rush. Better longer and clear than fast and confusing.
    • Pause before key phrases ("Always. Immediately. At the moment of opening.").
    • Look into the camera as if you're talking to a friend.
    • Don't read in a monotone — emphasise key words with intonation (marked in bold).
    • If you make a mistake — don't cut it out, keep going. Natural mistakes make the video feel more authentic.

    🔁 AFTER RECORDING¶

    • Don't upload it immediately. Rewatch it after 24 hours.
    • Write down what you understood anew while saying it out loud.
    • In 6 months, rewatch your video — you'll see how much you've grown.
